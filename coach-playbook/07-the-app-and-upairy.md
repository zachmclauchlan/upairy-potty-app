# The Potty Up app and UpAiry products

## What the app does (point parents to it when it helps)
- **The course:** 3-day intensive, bare bottom at home, gentle/child-led, nights, run as a day-by-day course from a start date (Day 1), with getting-ready lessons before it. Each day has a short lesson, checklist and words to say; days move on by themselves. At the end of a stage the app suggests moving on (from the logs) and the parent decides. In Program they can repeat a day, pause the course, go back a stage or change the start date. The evening check-in is optional.
- **Smart timer:** learns the child's rhythm from their logs, comes sooner after drinks and waking, and respects quiet hours. Use Pause for naps, car trips and outings.
- **Logging:** one tap for wee, poo, accident or tried, plus drinks, meals and wake-ups. Reminders have log buttons.
- **Kid zone:** stars, sticker chart, rewards the parent chooses, kid mode, a sit timer and potty songs.
- **Progress:** success trend, accident times, history calendar, night tracking.
- **Share an update:** a ready-to-send note for daycare or grandparents.
- **Readiness check:** 8 questions.
- Suggest a feature by name when it fits. For example:
  - "Log the next few days and the timer will learn their pattern."
  - "Use Pause for the car trip."
  - "The evening check-in will tell you whether to move on."
  - "Send daycare the update from Settings → Share an update."

## The UpAiry range: what to suggest, when, and how to use it

### Potty Training Underwear (UpAiry's core product)
**What they are:**
- Reusable cloth training pants that look and feel like real underwear.
- They feel wet, so the child notices, but absorbent layers hold a small accident so it doesn't run down their legs.
- Lots of fun prints (trucks, planes, unicorns, whales, puppies and more).
- Children often want to "keep the trucks dry".

**Sizes are by weight:**
- **S:** 3–9 kg (7–20 lb)
- **M:** 10–16 kg (22–35 lb). Most toddlers starting training.
- **L:** 17–25 kg (37–55 lb)
- If between sizes, **size down** (they're stretchy).
- A good fit is snug at the waist and legs, without gaps.
- Free returns and exchanges if the size is wrong.

**How many pairs:**
- **3-day or intensive start:** about 10 or more pairs, so you're not washing every few hours (the 10-pack or the Daytime Kit).
- **Gentle or gradual:** about 5–8 pairs.
- The app's pants planner shows how many you have against what your method needs.

**How to use them:**
- **Day 1:** let the child choose their print, and make putting them on a big moment.
- Use them like normal undies in the day. On Oh Crap-style plans, bring them in after the bare-bottom or commando stage.
- **After an accident:**
  1. Change calmly.
  2. Tip any poo into the toilet.
  3. Put the pair in the wet bag.
- Keep nappies or pull-ups for nap and night until those are often dry.
- **Use them as the "daycare alternative to pull-ups":** many centres accept cloth training pants because they contain small accidents.

**Washing:**
- Shake or rinse any poo into the toilet.
- Store in a wet bag until wash day.
- Wash with normal detergent, and avoid fabric softener (it reduces absorbency).
- Line dry or tumble on low. Follow the care label.
- Wash new pairs before first use.

### Extra Absorbent Inserts (bamboo charcoal)
- Pads that sit inside the training pants for **extra absorbency**.
- **Use them for:** naps, car trips, outings, daycare, or long stretches where a bigger accident would be a problem. Also on the first nappy-free nights.
- **Tip:** for everyday home training, leave the insert out so they still feel wet, and save inserts for "protection times".

### Daytime Potty Training Kit and 10-pack
- The easiest way to start a method with enough pairs, in boy or girl prints.
- **Suggest it** when a parent is about to start the 3-day, bare-bottom or gentle method and asks what they need.

### Nighttime Potty Training Kit
- **For:** going nappy-free at night. Pair with the bed mat.
- **Suggest it when:** mornings have been dry or nearly dry for 1–2 weeks, or the child asks to stop night nappies.

### Waterproof Bed Mat (+ mesh wash bag)
- Goes on the bed under the child, so a wet night means changing the mat, not the mattress.
- **Tip:** "double-make" the bed: mat, sheet, then a second mat and sheet. At 2 am you just strip the top layer.
- The mesh bag keeps small items together in the wash.

### Waterproof Wet Bags
- **Two compartments:** clean pairs in one, wet ones in the other.
- **For:** the nappy bag, daycare and outings. Zip closed, and no smell leaks.
- At home, keep one by the potty as the "wash day" bag.

### Toddler Toilet Step Stool
- For the big toilet: **feet flat, knees above hips**. This makes poos easier and helps children feel safe.
- **Especially useful for:** poo holding, constipation, fear of the big toilet, and boys learning to stand at the toilet.
- Pair with a child seat insert.

### Portable Potties (banana and whale folding potties)
- Fold flat for the car or bag. For outings, long drives, parks, and children scared of public toilets.
- **Tip:** practise with it at home first.

### Bedwetting Alarm
- For children **5 and over** who are still wetting at night.
- See the nights section for how to use one properly (every night, a parent helps at first, usually 6–8 weeks).
- Suggest a GP check first.
- Never promise it will work. It helps many children, about half to three-quarters.

### Potty Training Sticker Chart
- A rewards chart to stick on the wall (stickers not included).
- **Use it for:** effort, like sitting, telling you and wees in the potty. At night, reward behaviours like the bedtime wee, not dry nights.
- The app also has a sticker chart in kid mode.
