# What real parents say worked (and what backfired)

## Poo
- Treat constipation first. GP-guided stool softeners (Movicol, Laxido, lactulose) are often kept up for months, then weaned. Once stools are soft, the behaviour often follows.
- Fade the nappy:
  1. Nappy only in the bathroom.
  2. Poo in the nappy while sitting on the toilet.
  3. Cut a small hole, and make it bigger over weeks.
  4. Only the waistband left, then nothing.
  - Variation: a nappy laid in the bowl under the seat.
- Blowing bubbles or a toy only on the toilet (relaxes the pelvic floor).
- Feet supported and a secure seat (a family seat with a child ring, plus a sturdy step).
- Sit after meals at the child's usual poo time. Not rushed. Distraction helps some children, not all.
- Books: "Poo Goes to Pooland" (book, app and video) was often credited. Also NHS and ERIC stories.
- A small treat only for poo (or a bigger one for poo than for wee). Keep it low-key for anxious kids.
- If they hide to poo, move the potty to the hiding spot and edge it closer to the bathroom each day.
- A warm evening bath helps them relax.
- Backfired: forcing sits while crying, big reward escalations, treating it as defiance.

## Refusing to sit, fear, holding wee
- Skip the potty and go straight to the toilet with a seat insert and a step. This is the most common fix for "the potty is scary/babyish".
- Model it: narrate your own trips, and let them flush and wave bye-bye.
- A tiny reward just for sitting, so the potty stops being scary.
- Wee in the bath, a tepid shower, or outside on the grass helps a child who can't let go.
- Holding wee for hours or sobbing → stop and wait a few weeks (risk of fear, UTIs, constipation).

## Not noticing the urge
- Real underwear or thick cloth training pants rather than pull-ups, so they feel wet.
- Prompt about every hour or at transitions, not every 15–20 min. Stretch the interval over time.
- Log wee times and offer about 30 min before their usual times.
- Name their tells: going quiet, stopping play, dancing, holding themselves.
- Accidents are an inconvenience, not a punishment: a calm stock phrase, then they help strip off and put clothes in the wash. Use easy clothes a size up.
- Uses the potty when naked but forgets when dressed → go commando in loose trousers for 1–2 weeks.
- A wee game: wee into a measuring cup and read the number.

## Daycare and school
- Accidents at nursery are normal for weeks or months after home is reliable (busy, exciting, other kids in nappies, unfamiliar toilets).
- Agree a schedule with staff (every 1.5–2 h, or before snack and outdoor play) instead of "do you need to go?".
- Send the chart to nursery and give prizes at home. Pack lots of spare clothes and bags.
- Talk to the school early: they have intimate care plans and have seen it before.
- Good at nursery but bad at home → copy nursery's routine at home.
- Daycare rules like "two weeks accident-free before underwear" slow progress. Ask if they'll accept cloth training pants.

## Regression
- Stay calm and low-fuss. It often settles in about 2 weeks.
- One-to-one time with the older child, and involve them in the baby's care.
- A brief return to pull-ups after a new baby is okay. Most recover within weeks.
- A daily "dry pants" sticker for dribbling.
- Rule out constipation or a urine infection first (AAP).

## Outings
- A wee before leaving the house is part of going out.
- Start with an hour at the local park, then build up to cafés and soft play over about a week.
- Pack a travel potty, spare clothes, wipes, bags, and a puppy pad on the car seat.
- Pull-ups only for flights and long drives, worn over undies so they still feel wet. No setback reported.
- Tell café staff you're potty training. They'll often let you use the staff toilet.
- Auto-flush fear: a sticky note over the sensor. Hand-dryer fear: cover their ears or use ear defenders, and practise at home with a hairdryer they control.
- Sugary drinks mean very frequent wees.

## Boys
- Sit for everything first (it catches poos too). Stand later, at about 3–3.5, once fully trained.
- Tuck the penis down and sit them well back to stop spray.
- Sitting backwards on the toilet (facing the cistern) helps aim and balance. Nurseries use it.
- A boy who won't sit: let him stand, with a target in the bowl.

## Autism, ADHD, additional needs
- A now/next picture board ("toilet, then iPad"), social stories, and straight to the toilet rather than a potty.
- Physical security: a seat with handles and a sturdy step (an OT tip).
- Fade nappies gradually. A strongly wanted toy for the first 10 successes.
- An intimate care plan at school. Many children train at 5–7. Follow the child's pace.
