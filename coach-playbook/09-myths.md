# Myths the coach should gently correct

- **"If it doesn't work in 3 days, they're not ready / I failed."** No method beats the others for every child. Weeks to months is normal, and setbacks are part of learning.
- **"Never start until they ask."** Many children never show obvious signs. This is especially true of children with additional needs. UK continence experts (ERIC) suggest moving out of nappies between about 18 and 30 months for bladder and bowel health, while US sources lean more on readiness cues. Present both: readiness cues help, but you don't have to wait forever.
- **"There's a magic window: too young before 2, too late after 3."** The study often quoted (Hodges 2014) was small, and pointed to **constipation**, not age, as the real problem.
- **"Night pull-ups stop them learning to be dry at night."** Night dryness depends on a hormone and the brain waking to a full bladder. It can't be trained, and most children are dry at night by 5–6.
- **"Cut drinks to keep them dry."** This leads to a smaller bladder, urine infections and constipation. Drink well in the day, and stop about 1 hour before bed.
- **"Lift them to wee at night."** It's a short-term convenience, not training, and can teach weeing while asleep.
- **"Poo holding is stubbornness, so be firm or make them sit."** It's usually fear of pain from constipation. Soft poos plus low pressure fix it. Poo accidents after holding can be overflow, which needs a doctor, not discipline. Even daily poos can still mean constipation.
- **"Don't bother the GP about poo."** Constipation affects up to about 1 in 3 children, and is the first thing to check in any regression.
- **"Punish or shame accidents so they learn."** Every expert body says this slows learning. What works is a calm "wee goes in the potty", with the child helping to clean up as a job.
- **"Rewards always work" or "Rewards ruin it."** Small, immediate rewards help many children (especially just for poo, or just for sitting if they're scared). Big, escalating rewards backfire for anxious kids. Keep them small and fade them out.
- **"Ask every 15 minutes."** The child sits before the bladder is full, nothing comes, and they don't learn their own signal. Prompt at transitions or about every hour, and watch for their tells.
- **"Boys are just lazy."** Boys train a little later on average and hold poo more often. It isn't laziness. Let them sit first and stand later.
- **"Regression means they were never trained."** A new sibling, a move, starting daycare, illness, constipation or a urine infection are common triggers. It usually settles in days to weeks.
- **"Accidents at daycare mean daycare is doing it wrong, or the child is faking."** Busy new settings cause accidents for weeks to months. Fix it with scheduled prompts and easy access, not blame.
